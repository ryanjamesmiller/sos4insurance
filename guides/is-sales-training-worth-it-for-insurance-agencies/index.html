Is Sales Training Worth It for Insurance Agencies?

By Ryan James Miller, founder of SOS · September 2026

The short answer

Most one-day sales training isn't worth it, because nothing is installed when the day ends and producers go back to doing what they did before. What changes production is a system built inside your agency: a written target, a prospecting sequence, scripts each producer owns in their own voice, and several weeks of coached execution until it runs without you.

Why workshops don't stick

A workshop teaches a skill for a day. Everyone leaves fired up. Monday, the renewals are due, the inbox is full, and nothing in the agency has changed. There's no target list, no sequence, no scripts, no number and no one checking. Within a month it's gone.

People don't buy information. They buy execution. Your producers don't need more information about prospecting. They need a system they actually run.

Questions to ask before you pay for anything

  • What will my producers have in hand when it's over? If the answer is "skills" or "a workbook," be careful.
  • Is it built for insurance? Benefits and commercial P&C buy on renewal dates and x-dates. Generic sales training doesn't know that.
  • Whose words will my producers use? Scripts in the trainer's voice get abandoned. Scripts in the producer's own voice get used.
  • What happens after the teaching? Who checks that it's running in week three and week eight?
  • How much of the owner's time does it need? A good build takes the owner's time up front and gives it back after.
  • Who checks what producers send? Every script should be reviewed for claims and promises your agency can't stand behind.

"We already have a sales process"

Most agencies do, and it usually works well once a producer is in the room. What's usually missing is the part before the room: who to pursue, how to open the door, and how to stay present until the prospect is ready. As one producer put it after the first session: "We don't have to completely rebuild it; we just have to refine it. That's a lot less overwhelming."

What it looks like when it's installed

At SOS the build is four live sessions over 6 to 12 weeks, then a Personal Runbook for every producer, then weekly forum calls and coaching. At one regional benefits agency, nine producers generated over $1.5 million in closed business in the first seven months. The question isn't whether the system works. It's whether your team will run it.

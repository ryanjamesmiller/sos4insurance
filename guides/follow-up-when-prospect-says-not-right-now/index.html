How Should Insurance Producers Follow Up When a Prospect Says "Not Right Now"?

By Ryan James Miller, founder of SOS · September 2026

The short answer

Treat "not right now" as a timing answer, not a no. In insurance it's usually the renewal calendar talking. Send a recap within 24 hours, then run a planned sequence of value touches (a relevant case story, the problem explained, the approach, the results) and make one direct, low-pressure ask to reconnect at the end. Never send a "just checking in."

Why this is where deals die

A prospect says, "I like what you're saying, but now isn't the right time." Most producers hear a no, send one "just following up" email, and go quiet. Six months later the renewal opens and the prospect calls whoever stayed in front of them.

Here's the thing: the stall is almost always structural. The prospect can like everything you said and still be locked to a buying window that's months away. Your job is to still be the one they think of when that window opens.

Three shifts before you send anything

  • Silence isn't rejection. A quiet prospect is usually busy, talking to their current broker, or waiting for the right internal moment.
  • Follow-up is a service, not a reminder. Every touch should leave them better informed, whether or not they ever buy.
  • Trust compounds. The prospect who ignored your first email will often reply to your seventh, because by then you've proven you're different.

The sequence: 7 touches over 21 days

We call this the Value Cadence System.

TouchDayWhat it does
11Recap within 24 hours: what they told you, in their words, and the next step
23 to 4A case story tied to the exact frustration they shared
37The problem explained: what's really driving it. No ask
410The approach that worked, as a thought partner, not a vendor
514How it was actually rolled out, step by step
618The outcomes and numbers, tied to what they said they care about
721A short, direct, low-pressure ask to reconnect

Touch 5 is the one that most often gets a reply. When a prospect sees how the work actually gets done, they start to picture themselves as your client.

The 21 days is the engagement sequence, not the whole wait. If the renewal is seven months out, the sequence earns you the right to stay in touch until it opens.

If they still don't reply

A deal is only dead when the prospect says so. Shift to re-engagement: every so often, send something useful, like a market development, a relevant story or an observation about their business, tied to what they told you on the first call.

What never gets sent: "Just circling back to see if you had a chance to review what I sent." It asks for their time and gives nothing back. If you can't name what a message gives the prospect, don't send it.

When they come back

Don't jump to a proposal. Time has passed and things change. Start with a quick discovery refresh: confirm the problem still holds, re-confirm the numbers out loud, and then move forward.

Five standards for every deal in the pipeline

  • Every active deal has a next touch scheduled.
  • No check-ins. Value before every ask.
  • No deal goes dark. Quiet means re-engage now, not wait and hope.
  • Every touch is logged in the CRM.
  • Every message references something that prospect actually told you.

The producers who win aren't the ones with the best pitch. They're the ones who never let a good conversation die.

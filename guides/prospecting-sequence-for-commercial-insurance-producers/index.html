What Is a Good Prospecting Sequence for Commercial Insurance Producers?

By Ryan James Miller, founder of SOS · September 2026

The short answer

A good prospecting sequence is qualified, multi-channel, value-first and finite. The one we install is the 1440: 14 touches over 40 days across 6 channels, timed to the account's renewal date or x-date, with every touch delivering something useful before it asks for anything and a clear decision point on Day 40.

What makes a sequence good

  • Qualified. It only runs on accounts that fit your target and have a known or likely renewal or x-date coming. Forty days of effort on the wrong account is forty days lost.
  • Multi-channel. Email alone gets ignored. Calls alone get screened. Six channels make your name familiar before you ever connect.
  • Value-first. Every touch gives something: a case story, a market insight, a resource. Nothing is filler and nothing is "just checking in."
  • Finite. It ends on a set day with a defined next step for every account, so nobody sits in limbo.

The 1440, phase by phase

The name comes from the 1,440 minutes in every day. The question is never whether you have time. It's what you do with it.

The pinnacle touch

Touch 8 is what separates the 1440 from every other cadence. On Day 16 the producer sends something physical: a gift chosen for that prospect, or a book that's relevant to their business with a handwritten note and flagged chapters. Nobody else in their inbox did that.

Timing it to the x-date

Commercial accounts move when the policy comes up. Build your list around renewal dates and x-dates, and start early enough that the prospect already knows your name before the incumbent starts remarketing. Showing up the week of the renewal makes you one more quote. Showing up 40 days earlier with value makes you the advisor they remember.

What happens on Day 40

Every account leaves the sequence on one of three paths. No dead ends.

  • They responded. Book the discovery call within 48 hours.
  • "Not now." Ask what timeframe works better, lock a specific month, and move them to a 90-day nurture track, one useful touch a month.
  • No response. Thirty days of silence, then one attempt through a new channel or a different contact at the company. After 60 days, re-enter or archive.

What the 1440 is not

It's not spray-and-pray volume, and it's not permission to be annoying. It's not a guarantee either: it dramatically raises the odds of a conversation, and the conversation is still yours to earn. And it only works if you run all of it. Partial systems produce partial results.
